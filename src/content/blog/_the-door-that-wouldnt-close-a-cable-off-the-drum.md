# The Door That Wouldn't Close: A Cable Off the Drum

Some garage door calls sound worse than they are.

A homeowner reached out with a door that had stopped working. One side wasn't moving right, the door was sitting crooked, and the whole thing had just quit cooperating. From the outside looking in, it probably felt like something was seriously wrong.

It wasn't.

---

## What I Found

When I pulled up and took a look, the diagnosis was quick. The lift cable on one side had jumped off the drum — the small spool mounted at each end of the torsion spring shaft that the cable winds around as the door opens and closes.

When a cable comes off the drum, the door loses its balanced lift on that side. One side goes up, one side doesn't. The door tilts, binds, and stops. The opener hits resistance and quits. From a homeowner's perspective it looks like a mechanical disaster. From a technician's perspective it's one of the more straightforward fixes in the trade.

No broken springs. No snapped cables. No damaged tracks. Just a cable that had slipped where it shouldn't have.

---

## How It Happens

Cables don't usually jump drums for no reason. A few things can cause it — a spring that's losing tension, a cable that's gotten slightly slack over time, or a door that came down too hard or fast and let the cable go loose for a split second. That moment of slack is all it takes for the cable to unseat from the drum groove and coil incorrectly on the next cycle.

It can also happen when someone manually operates the door while the cable is under unusual stress, or when a door is forced when something is partially blocking it.

In this case the door had probably been accumulating slack gradually. These things rarely happen all at once.

---

## The Fix

First things first — the cable had to go back where it belonged.

I released the tension on the spring system, reseated the cable properly into the drum groove, and made sure it was tracking correctly before touching anything else. A cable that goes back on crooked is a cable that comes off again.

Once the cable was seated and the tension restored, I lowered the door and checked the level. It was sitting slightly off — one side was lower than the other, which told me the drum on the affected side needed adjustment to match the cable wind on the opposite side.

I adjusted the drum position on the shaft until the door sat level across the bottom. That part matters more than most people realize. A door that closes crooked puts uneven stress on the weatherstripping, the tracks, and the opener every single cycle. Getting it level isn't just cosmetic — it's mechanical.

Once the adjustment was dialed in I ran the door through several full cycles. Smooth open, clean close, sitting level on the floor. Done.

---

## In and Out

Start to finish, the job didn't take long. That's not me rushing — it's just the nature of a cable off the drum when everything else is in decent shape. The cable was intact, the drum was undamaged, the spring was fine. It was a reset job, and reset jobs go quickly when you know what you're looking at.

The homeowner had a working door again before they probably expected to.
